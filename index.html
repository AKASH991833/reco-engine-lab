How the engines work

Content-based filtering

Each movie is a vector of features: genres, decade, director style tags, and keywords. Your profile is the rating-weighted average of the movies you rated (likes pull it toward them, dislikes push it away). Movies are scored by cosine similarity to your profile. Great with few ratings; tends to recommend "more of the same".

Collaborative filtering

RecLab ships with 60 synthetic users who rated the catalog. It finds the users whose taste matches yours (Pearson correlation over movies you both rated), then predicts your rating for each unseen movie as a similarity-weighted average of your neighbors' ratings. It can surprise you - but needs overlap to work.

Hybrid

A weighted blend of the two normalized scores. Slide the weight on the Compare tab to feel the trade-off in real time.

Honest limits

The catalog is 120 well-known movies and the other users are synthetic personas, so "collaborative" here demonstrates the math, not a live community. Real systems also fight cold start, popularity bias and filter bubbles - you can watch mild versions of all three here.
